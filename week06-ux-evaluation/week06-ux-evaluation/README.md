# Week 6 — UX Evaluation Brief

## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Task Walkthrough

Briefly describe what Maya would try to do first, what she would look for, and where she might hesitate.

[Maya would first try to find a search option to view beginner level trails, she would then look for information regarding the trial details and information on time of the trails length, and then she would hesitate if the information is not given in one easy to read, short, informative section. ]

---

## 2. Five UX Findings

Document **exactly five meaningful findings**.

### Finding 1

**Observation:**  
[What did you observe?]

I observed no immediate way to reach the first goal of searching for a trail

**Evidence:**  
[Where/how did it appear?]

This appeared in the call to action/hero section at the top of the page

**User Impact:**  
[How could this affect Maya's task?]

This could impacy Maya's task by convoluting her first step of searching for a trail with easy difficulty

**Principle:**  
[Usability/accessibility principle.]

This impacts the usability/accessibility of the site by not immediately guiding user attention to the first important step of finding a trail, which reduces user retention if they don't have an easy time finding what they are looking for

**Priority:** Low / Medium / High

High priority as first user impressions are the most important, especially when they guide the user immediately into making a decision

**Recommendation:**  
[Describe the improvement at a high level. Do not design the final solution yet.]

My recommendation would be to implement a call to action button, which moves users to a list of trails, with a criteria search, as well as to enlarge the section to make the users not feel bombarded with options by seeing other sections, and to reduce the options of the top navbar to give the CTA more emphasis

### Finding 2

**Observation:**

Unclear where to find trail details regarding time of completion and difficulty

**Evidence:**

In the featured trails section within each card

**User Impact:**

This impacts Maya's second task of finding information about the time it will take to complete a hike, and whether or not it will be easy to traverse, making them less inclined to keep searching if the process already has had too much friction

**Principle:**

This impacts the usability/accessibility of the site by adding friction to the user experience since information is not easily accessible and all in one place

**Priority:**

High priority as this will lead the user to most likely leave the site if they cannot easily find the information they are looking for when searching for trails

**Recommendation:**

My recommendation would be to clarify what the color and text means at the top of the card so they know that it means the difficulty of the trails, and to add a time of completion next to the difficulty so they know immediately how long the trail will take to complete

### Finding 3

**Observation:**

Unclear elements defining the trails

**Evidence:**

In each of the trail cards, next to the length of the trail there is a number of feet that is not clarified as to what it means

**User Impact:**

This impacts Maya's decision by confusing them with unclear information about the hike, especially since she would like to know the elevation details to know how much climbing is involved

**Principle:**

This impacts the usability/accessibility of the site by making informative cards harder to read, and reduces clarity which will impact user decision making

**Priority:**

Medium priority, this is likely useful information, but not vital to the overall flow and usability of the site

**Recommendation:**

My recommendation would be to clarify what exactly that number is for by stating next to it, if it is for elevation or other features of the trail

### Finding 4

**Observation:**

No GPS loction or map for trail is included in information

**Evidence:**

In the more info section at the bottom of the page by scrolling, or by clicking "More info" button on the trail cards

**User Impact:**

This impacts Maya's decision by leaving her with desired information, especially since she likes to screenshot important information, such as a map, in case the service is poor, and also since she likes to view photographs first before reading

**Principle:**

This impacts the usability/accessibility of the site by not allowing users to view the trail beforehand, and to determine how far away the trail is, and where to find it

**Priority:**

High priority, if this information is not provided, users will struggle to know where the trail is, how long it will take to get to the trail, and access to a map for when service is spotty while on the trail

**Recommendation:**

In the more details section, add GPS information, and a map of the trail for users to screenshot or download

### Finding 5

**Observation:**

Dog information is not easily accessible, or not provided at all

**Evidence:**

No information or icon for if dogs are allowed found in the trail card section, and in the more information section, dog info is only provided for Juniper Creek Loop trail

**User Impact:**

This impacts Maya's decision by not being able to quickly determine if dogs are allowed on the trail

**Principle:**

This impacts the usability/accessibility of the site by not providing easy to see information, and provides conflict for dog lovers that will always want to take their dogs out with them

**Priority:**

Medium priority, this information can be very valuable to certain users that go out with their pets, but does not prevent the user from completing the main task of finding a trail

**Recommendation:**

My recommendation would be to add an icon of a dog to the trail cards, and either a checkmark or an X to let users know if dogs are allowed or not
